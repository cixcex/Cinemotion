# Environment Design

Created and maintained by Aditya (@cixcex).

An environment must feel inhabited. Avoid empty stages.

## Every environment includes

- Spatial structure: floor, walls, ceiling or sky, exits.
- Light sources: lamps, signs, windows, screens.
- Key objects: furniture, vehicles, signs, props.
- Background details: posters, cables, shelves, weather.
- Atmosphere: fog, rain, dust, steam, haze.
- Camera placement: where the camera starts and ends.

## Presets

### Bedroom

- Bed, lamp, window, phone, rug, posters.
- Warm lamp + cool window light.
- Camera at bedside eye level.

### Convenience store at night

- Shelves, cooler doors, checkout counter, fluorescent tubes, glass entrance.
- Cold interior light spilling onto wet pavement.
- Camera outside looking in, or POV entering.

### Rainy neon street

- Wet road, puddles, signs, vending machines, buildings.
- Neon reflections, volumetric haze, drizzle particles.
- Low camera for reflections; eye level for walking.

### Rooftop

- Parapet, vents, skyline, blinking antenna light.
- Dusk gradient, rim from city.
- Wide static or slow dolly toward edge.

## Environmental activity

Add one subtle living element:

- Flickering sign.
- Steam from a vent.
- Rain ripples.
- Refrigerator hum light.
- Distant traffic glow.

## Low-cost build order

1. Floor and walls.
2. Main light source.
3. Two or three key props.
4. One atmospheric detail.
5. Camera and one moving element.

## Output rule

Return the environment description with camera placement and lighting so the engine code can be generated without another round-trip.
